# Changelog

## Unreleased

- feat(web): **a sound panel in the player bar**: the new sliders button (next to the volume, also on phones) opens your own sound settings, kept in this browser like repeat and shuffle; everyone hears their own. A dot on the button shows when something changes the sound
- feat(web): **playback speed**, 0.5× to 2×, with the pitch kept. While you listen along with someone the music plays at 1× and your speed comes back when you stop; when the person you follow changes their speed, you are no longer pulled back every few seconds (their song changes, pauses and seeks still reach you). The lock screen's progress follows the speed
- feat(web): **sleep timer**: stop after 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, a time you type (minutes or h:mm) or at the end of the song. The panel counts down and a small moon on the button shows it is set; the music fades out over a few seconds and pauses, exactly as if you paused it (listening along with someone ends; people listening along with you pause too). It keeps running while you move around the app; reloading the page cancels it
- feat(web): **equalizer and volume boost**: ten bands with presets (Bass boost, Treble boost, Vocal / speech, Rock, Electronic, Classical / acoustic, Small speakers) or your own curve, and a boost up to twice as loud with a limiter so loud songs do not distort. With both off nothing changes in how music plays. On phones and tablets they stay off until you switch them on there, because the browser can stop such audio when the screen locks
- feat(web): **skip silence**, on by default: songs start where the music starts and move on where it ends, skipping the seconds of nothing many video uploads have at either end. Quiet intros are kept; seeking into the silence by hand plays it; not while listening along. Turn it off in the panel
- feat(api): library songs carry `lead_silence_ms` and `trail_silence_ms` (silence at the very start / end in ms, `null` until measured; additive, older clients ignore them); `GET /api/stream/silence?q=<queue url>` answers them for a queue entry's song. The server measures new and replaced uploads at once and the existing library in the background, one song at a time at low priority (`SILENCE_SCAN=off` stops it); admins see the progress at `GET /api/admin/library/silence`. `presence:now-playing` / `listen:sync` carry the player's `rate`

- feat(web): **folders for your playlists, like Spotify.** Make a folder with the new folder button above the playlists, then put playlists in it from their ⋮ menu (**Move to folder ▸**, which can also make a new folder) or, with a mouse, by dragging them onto it; drag one out, or choose **Remove from …**, to take it out again. Folders open and close (remembered in this browser), can be renamed and deleted (their playlists go back to the list, none is deleted), and hold any playlist you can see, yours or public ones; up to 100. They are yours alone: nobody else sees them, they change nothing about the playlists, and your other tabs and devices follow at once. A pinned playlist stays in Pinned too. All and Mine show the folders at the top of the list, Public and Private and a search list everything with "in <folder>" (a folder whose name matches the search opens), the sort applies inside folders, and the collapsed sidebar shows each folder as one icon that opens it. A playlist that becomes private, or is deleted, leaves your folder; one that comes back is back in it. For apps: `/api/playlist-folders` (web and Android app sessions)
- feat(web): **search in Radio.** A field above Radio's songs filters them as you type by title, artist or album ("barıs manco" finds "Barış Manço"), with "2 of 2,108 songs" beside it. It only changes what you see: a song you pick from the results plays and Radio goes on through the whole shuffled station after it, and typing never interrupts what is playing. Esc or Clear search shows everything again
- feat(android): **downloaded playlists that stay in sync, like Spotify.** A playlist's new **Download** switch (on its page, and in its ⋮ menu) keeps every song of it on the phone, and songs added to it later — by anyone, on the web or another phone — download by themselves: when the app opens, when you refresh, when the connection comes back, and every 6 hours in the background. Downloaded playlists and songs carry a small green mark, a ring while downloading; each playlist says what it is doing (downloaded with its size, "Downloading 3 of 12", "Waiting for Wi-Fi", "Paused while offline", a Retry when something failed). Playlists has a **Downloaded** filter, and On device lists your downloaded playlists first, then the songs you downloaded on their own. A playlist that is deleted or made private keeps its songs and shows "No longer available"
- feat(android): **songs removed from a downloaded playlist are removed from the phone too**, on by default (Settings → "Remove downloads of songs removed from a playlist"). Only files the app downloaded for a playlist go, never a song you downloaded yourself, never one another downloaded playlist still holds, and never anything downloaded before this version. A playlist that suddenly comes back empty or loses more than half its songs at once removes nothing until the next refresh says the same. Turning a playlist's Download off asks whether to keep its songs or remove them
- feat(android): **playlists also download over mobile data**, on by default (Settings → "Download over mobile data"; off = Wi-Fi only). Never while roaming or with Data Saver on. Songs you download yourself are not affected
- feat(android): **covers.** Every song shows its cover in lists, the player, the notification, the lock screen and Android Auto (the same pictures as on the website). Downloaded songs keep theirs for offline use — saved privately by the app, not as extra files in your Music folder — and songs downloaded earlier get theirs the next time the phone is online. Covers are loaded without your sign-in being sent anywhere
- feat(android): **a notice when a newer version is out.** "Version X.Y.Z is available" with Download (opens the release in the browser) and Later (asks again in 3 days, or at once for an even newer one). Never forced. Settings → About has **Check for updates**
- change(android): **the app looks like the website on a phone**: rounded panels on the dark page with small gaps (the page, the player bar, the tabs), the website's segmented tabs (Library's All songs / Broken, Playlists' All / Mine / Public / Private), each playlist's coloured cover with its initials — the same colour as on the website —, the playing song tinted with an equaliser, and the website's thin seek bar
- change(android): Android Auto lists downloaded playlists first, marked as downloaded; they are there with every song when the phone is offline

- change(web): **the sidebar and the chat column reach the top of the window again**, as on Spotify: the search field now sits at the top of the middle column, centred over the page instead of over the whole window, and the side panels run the full height from the top down to the player bar. Phones are unchanged
- feat(dev): **one command for a complete local setup**, `make up` (`make down`, `make reset`): an Aspire AppHost written in TypeScript (`aspire/apphost.mts`; needs the Aspire CLI and Docker, no .NET SDK) starts PostgreSQL 16 with a kept data volume, the server in watch mode, the frontend compiler in watch mode and a seed with two accounts, five short songs, three playlists and some chat, then the dev sign-in works at once. The Aspire dashboard shows each part's logs and the server's traces and metrics. `LISTEN_WORKER=1` adds a downloader (`la worker`), `LISTEN_AUTH=oidc` signs in through the repo's fake OpenID provider instead. Development only: the image, the compose files and CI do not use it (README "Local development")

- feat(ops): **OpenTelemetry for the server, off by default.** With an OTLP endpoint configured (`NODE_OPTIONS=--require=/app/src/telemetry.js` and `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT`, OTLP over HTTP), the server sends a trace per request, named by its route (`GET /api/playlists/:id`), with its Postgres queries and outgoing calls underneath, plus HTTP, database pool and Node runtime metrics; log lines written during a request carry its `trace_id`. Nothing private is sent: no bodies, headers, cookies, query strings (the sign-in callback's code included), client addresses, SQL values, emails or chat text, and `/health`, static files and Socket.IO polling are not traced. Without an endpoint nothing is loaded or started, so production is unchanged until it is switched on (README "OpenTelemetry"). The image's `node_modules` grows by about 16 MB
- feat(android): **the app signs in like the web: no more tokens.** Settings (and the empty Library and Playlists) has a **Sign in** button that opens the central login's page in the browser; it comes back to the app by itself, and when you are already signed in there it is one tap. Your account is found exactly as on the web (the same rules, the same "Registrations are closed", "Your account has been disabled" and "contact the administrator" answers). The app keeps only a session of its own, stored encrypted on the phone; it never sees the central login's tokens. **Sign out** is in Settings, under Account. The session is checked with the central login like a browser's, so an account disabled there or rejected in `/dashboard` is signed out at once, and it lasts 30 days. Only a definite "signed out" from the server sends you back to Sign in: offline, with the server down or unreachable, downloaded songs keep playing and nothing signs you out. Changing the server address asks you to sign in again there
- change(android): **the API token is gone from the app.** The token field, Paste, "Open my profile" and "Save and test" are replaced by Sign in / Sign out / Test connection. A token saved by an older version is deleted when the new one starts (it still works for the CLI until you revoke it on your profile page); downloads, saved playlists and settings are kept
- feat(api): the app's sign-in: `GET /auth/mobile/start` (on to the central login with the app's registered redirect), `POST /auth/mobile/exchange` (`{ code, state, code_verifier, nonce }` → `{ token, token_type, expires_at, mode, user }`, or `{ error, code }`: `invalid_request`, `signin_failed`, `account_disabled`, `registrations_closed`, `link_conflict`, `signin_unavailable`), `POST /auth/mobile/logout`. A development server (`INSECURE_DEV_LOGIN`) shows a "Continue as Dev User" page instead of the IdP, which only debug builds of the app accept

- feat(web): a song that is itself broken is **marked as needing replacement automatically**, in your name, once another song has played after it. Broken means the server has lost that one file, or the file does not decode: for that the player fetches the whole file again and plays it silently from memory, so a dropped connection or a switched audio device is never mistaken for a broken song. Before the report is sent, the server is asked once more about the broken song and about the one now playing. And the server has the last word: it checks the song itself (the file is gone while other songs' files are there, or its own decoder cannot read the file) and stores no report otherwise, so a browser, cache or audio-device problem cannot mark a good song. The toast has **Undo**, and "Unmark broken" in the song menu withdraws it like any report. Nothing is reported when you are offline, when the server or the connection fails, when your session expired, when the server's music storage is missing, when the browser cannot play the format (Opus on an older Safari), when the file is over 64 MB and cannot be checked, when several songs fail in a row, or while listening along.
